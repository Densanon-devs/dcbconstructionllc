Tile Installation in Hayden, Idaho

Tile floors, walls, showers, backsplashes, and fireplace surrounds — installed right the first time for Hayden homes.

Tile is the difference between a remodel that looks great for a year and one that looks great for 20. The substrate prep, waterproofing, and grout work are what determine whether tile lasts — and they're exactly the steps that get rushed by less-experienced installers. DCB Construction LLC has been installing tile in Hayden homes for over a decade — ceramic, porcelain, natural stone, mosaic, large-format — across floors, walls, showers, backsplashes, and fireplace surrounds.

We work with the material you want and the substrate you have. We don't shortcut the underlayment, we don't paint over uneven subfloors, and we don't grout with the wrong product for the application.

Tile Installation in Hayden

Hayden brings us more natural stone and more heated floor than either of the other two towns, largely because of the size and specification of the homes around the lake and north of the centre.

Natural stone is a different material to work with than porcelain and it behaves differently once it is down. Marble, travertine and slate vary from piece to piece, which is much of the appeal, but it means the layout has to be dry-laid and blended across the whole floor before anything is set rather than pulled tile by tile out of the box. Stone is also porous: it needs sealing on installation and periodically afterwards, and some stones will not tolerate acidic cleaners at all. None of that is a reason to avoid it, but it should be a decision made with the maintenance in view.

Electric floor heat is the other request we get most often here. It goes down as a cable or a mat set into an uncoupling membrane beneath the tile, which means it has to be planned alongside the tile rather than added later. Retrofitting a heated floor means pulling the finished floor up. If the floor is already coming out for other reasons, it is the cheapest it will ever be to add.

Large-format and slab-look porcelain is common in the newer Hayden builds and gives you much of the appearance of stone with none of the sealing. It puts the same demand on substrate flatness that any large tile does, so the prep does not get any shorter.
