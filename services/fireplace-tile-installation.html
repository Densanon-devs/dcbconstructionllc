Fireplace Tile & Surround Installation

Surrounds, hearths, and floor-to-ceiling feature walls — built to the clearances your appliance actually requires.

Fireplace Tile Work We Do

A fireplace is usually the first thing people look at when they walk into a room, and it is one of the few remodels that changes a space without moving a single wall. It is also the tile job with the least margin for error: the layout is at eye level, the focal point is dead centre, and every cut shows.

  • Tile and stone surrounds around the firebox opening
  • Floor-to-ceiling feature walls, including vaulted rooms
  • Hearth construction and hearth extensions
  • Refacing dated brick without a full tear-out
  • Stacked stone and ledger panel installation
  • Mantel and trim integration as part of the same job

Materials That Handle the Heat

Tile and stone are noncombustible, which is exactly why they belong around a fire. What varies is how they behave over a surface that heats and cools every day through a Northern Idaho winter.

Porcelain and ceramic are the most predictable option: tight size tolerances, very low water absorption, and large-format panels that give you a continuous marble look with almost no grout. Natural and stacked stone bring depth and shadow that porcelain cannot really fake, at the cost of more variation piece to piece and a slower set. Glass and metal mosaics work well as an accent band but are less forgiving directly at the opening.

Whichever material goes on, the substrate underneath does the real work. Movement, a flexing panel, or a substrate that was never flat will telegraph through the finish long before the tile itself fails.

Clearances, Hearths, and Getting It Right

This is where fireplace tile differs from every other tile job in the house, and where most of the problems we get called to fix started.

Every wood stove, insert, and gas fireplace is sold with a listing that specifies minimum clearances to combustible material. That listing governs the whole assembly — not just the tile, but the framing behind it, the mantel above it, and the trim beside it. A beautiful surround that crowds the opening past what the appliance allows is a failed inspection at best.

We read the appliance documentation before the design is settled, build to whatever the manufacturer and your local inspector require, and leave the rating label accessible instead of burying it behind stone. Where a hearth extension is required, its depth comes from the appliance, not from what looks balanced.

How the Job Runs

  1. Walkthrough. We look at the existing fireplace, identify the appliance, and pull its clearance requirements.
  2. Layout and material. We plan the courses so the focal point lands where it should and you are not left with a sliver cut at the ceiling or beside the opening.
  3. Demo and prep. Old facing comes off where it needs to. The substrate gets flattened, and noncombustible backer goes on where required.
  4. Setting. Tile or stone goes on with the right mortar for the material and the orientation.
  5. Grout, seal, and finish. Natural stone gets sealed. Mantel and trim go on last.
  6. Cleanup. The room comes back to you usable.
